Little Tide

A quiet coastal almanac for iPhone.

Tide apps are usually dashboards for boaters. Little Tide is for people who just want to know when the tide is out at their beach, and to feel the shape of the day: one coast, one honest number, a timeline you can drag with your finger, and Home Screen widgets that stay true to NOAA's published predictions.

01 — Overview

A postcard, not a dashboard.

The home screen is a framed illustration of the chosen coast, a serif place name and one headline fact: the next high or low. Everything else is quieter. The illustration is drawn in code, one of six regional characters, and its waterline is set by NOAA's predicted height, so at low water the rocks and pilings show, and at high water they're covered.

The timeline is the app. Dragging across the day moves a marker, updates the readout and raises or lowers the drawn water, with a soft haptic at each real high and low.

02 — Engineering

Truthfulness as a design material.

Live probing found that NOAA's local-time output skips daylight saving for some Alaska stations, and that some metadata offsets are wrong. So Little Tide fetches times in GMT and resolves each station's civil IANA time zone itself. It asks for the 15-minute curve only from reference stations; subordinate stations show stems at their published events, never an invented curve.

All of this runs through a pure, clock-injected TideEngine, tested across DST changes, midnight and year boundaries, uneven event counts, gaps, negative heights and malformed data. The catalogue of 3,228 US stations is bundled with the app, so search works offline, and predictions are cached and labelled when they're stale.

Widgets that refresh at the right moments.

Each widget timeline entry is a pure piece of content with its own compositions per size. Entries come every 30 minutes and one second after each tide event, and the timeline reloads at the earlier of six hours or the last known event. When the Home Screen is tinted or clear, the scene redraws as single-colour line art in system colours.
