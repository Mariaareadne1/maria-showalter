Orbit

Draw paths for sounds to move around your head.

Orbit is a small spatial-sound stage. Up to six sounds sit around a listener; you place them, draw paths for them, or perform their motion live. Those positions drive Web Audio’s HRTF panner, and the arrangement renders offline to a WAV that pans the way playback did.

01 — Overview

Composing where a sound is, not just what it is.

Stereo panning puts a sound somewhere on a line between two speakers. With headphones and a head-related transfer function, a sound can sit to the side, move past you, or circle you. Orbit makes that a compositional parameter you can draw.

Three sounds are generated in code (drone, plucks, pulse); you can add your own files, which are decoded locally and never uploaded.

02 — Interaction

Place, draw, perform, export.

  1. Place

    Drag a sound to a fixed spot on a top-down room measured in metres.

  2. Draw

    Sketch a path; it is spread evenly across the piece, with direction marks on the stage.

  3. Perform

    Record your hand against the playing music; the score marks the stretch being replaced.

  4. Export

    Render the same graph offline to a 48 kHz, 16-bit stereo WAV, with peak and clipping reported.

03 — Engineering

Positions are scheduled on the audio clock.

Paths are piecewise-linear keyframes. Instead of moving the panner from the animation loop, Orbit schedules each keyframe on the PannerNode’s position parameters ahead of time with linearRampToValueAtTime. Motion is sample-accurate and independent of frame rate; the stage only reads the same interpolation to draw.

Live playback runs at 48 kHz like the export, because browser HRTF kernels depend on the sample rate.

Schedule ahead; seek exactly.

Moving a panner from requestAnimationFrame would tie spatial motion to frame rate and drop steps when the tab is busy. Scheduling on the audio clock removes that, but seeking then needs care: segments already in the past are replaced by the interpolated position at the seek offset. Loops are scheduled a quarter-second before each cycle ends.

src/audio/graph.ts · Orbit @ 3beba22

export function schedulePath(panner, path, startAt, offset = 0) {
  const params = [panner.positionX, panner.positionY, panner.positionZ]
  for (const p of params) p.cancelScheduledValues(0)
  setPosition(panner, positionAt(path, offset), startAt)
  for (const k of remaining(path, offset)) {
    const a = stageToAudio(k)
    const when = startAt + (k.t - offset)
    panner.positionX.linearRampToValueAtTime(a.x, when)
    // … y, z
  }
}

Export must pan the way playback did.

The export builds the same graph in an OfflineAudioContext. A repository test taps the live master with an AudioWorklet during a left-to-right pass, renders the same arrangement offline, and compares left/right balance in half-second windows.

05 — Status

Limitations and what isn’t shipped.

Spatial effects need headphones. Browser HRTF datasets differ by engine and are not personalised, so front/back and elevation cues are weak for many listeners; Orbit does not promise “8D” immersion. Sources stay at ear height. Firefox was not tested.
