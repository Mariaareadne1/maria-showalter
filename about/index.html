About

Maria Carla Ariadne Showalter

Computer Science at Barnard College of Columbia University, Hellenic Studies minor, class of 2028. I build software, music tools and photographs, and I don’t think of them as separate.

I build at the intersection of software, systems, and the things I want to exist in the world. Right now that means reinforcement learning, agentic AI, and a location-discovery app called Filo — built from my dorm room in React Native and Supabase because the best discovery tool I had was a screenshot of someone’s group chat.

My technical instincts were shaped by an unusual path. I grew up multilingual — Greek first, then English, French, Mandarin — and I came to code the same way I came to language: as a system with rules, and creativity as what you do within them. I’ve implemented REINFORCE policy gradient from scratch in PyTorch, built PID controllers for physical control problems, and increased production throughput from 3k to 18k units/day as a systems intern. I debug things until I understand them.

I’m Cherokee (my name is Copper Sky) and Greek American, which means I grew up holding two strong traditions at once — and got good at finding the structure underneath unfamiliar systems. I applied that the same way freshman year when I founded the Columbia Pickleball Club with no members, no equipment, and no budget. It grew to 200+ people. We got sponsored rackets. We built a pipeline to engineering internships at pickleball startups. I just wanted someone to play with.

I’m interested in agents that act in the world, systems that learn, and the places where engineering stops being purely functional. I hosted Columbia’s first Algorave — live coding music in front of an audience, projected on screen. I shoot on 35mm film. I think Bach and FM synthesis are the same lesson. These aren’t separate from the technical work. They’re why I do it.

I DJ with a Pioneer DDJ-400 running Serato, and perform with Strudel (a JavaScript port of TidalCycles).
