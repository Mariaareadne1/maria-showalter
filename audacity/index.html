We met as freshman roommates at Barnard — and three years later we still live together. We grew up worlds apart: Maria at an all-girls school in Baltimore, Gabriela in Mexico by way of a United World College in Bosnia. Neither of us ever heard the words "venture capital" growing up.

We both fell into the startup world the same way — by accident, through Columbia's entrepreneurship club — and had the same reaction: why is no one telling girls about this sooner? The Audacity Project is the program we wish someone had handed us at sixteen: real knowledge about startups and VC, and deliberate practice at the bold asks this world runs on.

The program · fall 2026

Eight sessions. Eight acts of audacity.

Every session pairs real startup and VC knowledge with a dare — a concrete act of boldness assigned as homework. Cold emails get sent. Founders get DMed. By pitch day, going for it feels normal. That's the point.

The audacity gap, the numbers behind it, and our founders' story. Every girl writes down the thing she's been "too unqualified" to try — sealed in an envelope until the final session.

Dare 01Write the thing you've talked yourself out of. Seal it. You'll meet it again.

Startup vs. small business vs. corporation — mapped through the apps already on your phone. What a founder actually does on a Tuesday.

Dare 02Cold email a founder. One sentence. The win is pressing send.

Business models in plain language — subscriptions, marketplaces, ads. Why a company can lose money and still be valuable.

Dare 03Name a problem you've lived, and commit to a number someone would pay to fix it.

Where startup money comes from, why investors fund unprofitable companies, and what owning a slice of something means. The session our founders wish they'd had.

Dare 04Find a woman at a VC firm. Send the LinkedIn request. Just connect.

Rounds, valuations, and the pizza-slice version of equity. A fictional founder's journey from idea to Series A.

Dare 05DM a founder who just raised. Congratulate her. You don't need a reason.

Teams form around real problems. One-line value propositions. An MVP with zero code — a mockup, a form, a single slide that makes someone get it in 30 seconds.

Dare 06Talk to one real potential user. Not a friend. Ask what would make it better.

Problem, solution, why you, why now. How to talk about things that aren't built yet. The confidence trick: you act first, the feeling follows.

Dare 07Pitch someone who'll push back. Report the hardest question you got.

Teams pitch to a panel of women in VC and founders — real feedback, not just applause. Then the envelopes from session one come back out, and every girl reads what she once thought she couldn't do.

Dare 08Open the envelope. See how far you've come.

For VCs & founders

Your pipeline problem starts in high school. So do we.

If you want more women founding companies and writing checks, the earliest intervention wins. We've proven the demand — now we're building the program to meet it. Partner with us and meet the next generation a decade before everyone else does.

  • i.Speak. 20 minutes on a video call with a session, or a seat on the pitch day panel.
  • ii.Host. Open your office to the NYC trip — a tour, a talk, a moment that makes it real.
  • iii.Sponsor. Fund the program and the trip. A small check here changes a trajectory.
